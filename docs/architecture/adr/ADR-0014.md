# ADR-0014 — Three tables are duplicated on purpose; each is owned by the thing that reads it

Status: Accepted
Date: 2026-09-20

## Context

Three pairs of near-identical tables live in this repo, and every one of them looks like an obvious
deduplication to a reader — or an agent — who happens to encounter one half of it. Two lists of
merchants. Two sets of calendar helpers. Two sets of spend categories that must never be touched.
Each pair already carries a source comment arguing for itself, but a comment is only read by
someone who opens that file, and the person most likely to merge the halves is the person reading
the *other* one.

The reason to write it down once, centrally, is that the protection is uneven. Only one of the
three is defended by anything mechanical. The other two would merge cleanly, pass CI, and change
behaviour in a way whose cause is invisible at the call site.

## Decision

All three stay duplicated. Each table is owned by the thing that reads it, not by whichever package
declared it first.

**1. Merchant recognition is not merchant generation.** `MERCHANTS`
(`packages/core/src/merchants.ts:29`) is a `readonly MerchantRule[]` of uppercase match tokens, used
to turn `UPI/SWIGGY/412683940281` into Swiggy and Swiggy into food delivery. `DISCRETIONARY`
(`packages/fixtures/src/merchants.ts:88`) is a `Record<string, readonly Merchant[]>` keyed by city,
carrying Gamma ticket means, MCCs and VPAs, used to invent transactions. Sharing one table would
make every categorisation test circular — the generator marking its own homework — and would hide
the case that actually matters in production, the merchant nobody has seen before. Core's table
deliberately lists merchants the generator never emits. They are not even the same type: unifying
them is a rewrite, not a rename.

**2. Core's calendar helpers are not imported from fixtures.** `packages/core/src/dates.ts`
duplicates the arithmetic in `packages/fixtures/src/calendar.ts` rather than importing it, because
core may not depend on the fixtures: the production build would ship synthetic customers, and the
dependency would point the wrong way — fixtures are data *for* the domain, not part of it.

**3. `NOT_CHALLENGEABLE` is not `NEVER_DISCRETIONARY`.** `NOT_CHALLENGEABLE`
(`packages/core/src/challenge.ts:71`) overlaps six categories with the set `derive()` holds
privately as `NEVER_DISCRETIONARY` (`packages/core/src/derive.ts:408`). Exporting the private one
would make it part of the Snapshot's contract, so a change made for the Snapshot's sake — narrowing
what counts as discretionary spending in a month, say — would silently change which challenges the
wizard offers, which is a different question with a different answer. `NOT_CHALLENGEABLE`
additionally carries `Rent & bills` and `Transfers`, which the private set does not: a challenge to
spend less on rent before the 1st is a threat rather than a challenge, and `Transfers` is the one
row on the statement whose purpose the bank genuinely does not know.

## Alternatives

**One shared merchant table**, with the generator and the categoriser reading the same rows. Cheap
to build and it makes the categorisation suite prove nothing: every narration the tests categorise
was emitted from the same table that categorises it.

**`packages/fixtures/src/dates.ts` imported by core**, deleting core's copy. Rejected here for the
reason ADR-0002 rejected it for the as-of arithmetic: the dependency points the wrong way, and the
production bundle gains the persona generator.

**Exporting `NEVER_DISCRETIONARY` from `derive.ts`** and having `challenge.ts` extend it. This is
the closest call of the three, because the six shared categories really are the same six. It is
rejected because the export is the damage: the moment that set is public it is part of the
Snapshot's contract, and the two questions — "what did this customer discretionarily spend?" and
"what may a customer set a ceiling on?" — stop being able to move independently.

## Consequences

Enforcement is asymmetric, and that is the operationally useful thing to know about this decision.

- **(2) is a CI failure.** `.dependency-cruiser.cjs:16-22`, the rule
  `core-imports-nothing-outside-itself`, forbids `packages/core/src` importing anything outside
  itself, and `apps/api/test/architecture/depcruise.test.ts` runs it on every build. Nobody can
  undo this duplication quietly.
- **(1) and (3) have no mechanical guard at all.** `fixtures → core` imports are permitted, so
  nothing stops the merchant tables being merged in core's direction, and (3) is entirely inside
  `packages/core` where nothing stands between the two sets. For those two, this ADR and the source
  comments at `packages/core/src/merchants.ts:9` and `packages/core/src/challenge.ts:51` are the
  whole protection — which is why they are written here as well as inline, and why `CONTEXT.md`
  points at all three.
- **The accepted failure mode for (1) and (3) is drift.** Two lists that agree today may not agree
  in six months, and nothing will fail when they diverge. That is the lesser evil against a feature
  that changes for a reason nobody can see.
- Anyone narrowing one of the three should widen the other deliberately, in the same commit, with
  the reason in the message.
