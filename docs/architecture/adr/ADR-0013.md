# ADR-0013 — Two interchangeable avatar providers behind one port; the gate moves to HTTP for Anam

Status: Accepted · amended 2026-09-20 (see Amendment)
Date: 2026-09-20

## Context

Runway's credits ran out mid-build, which turned a supplier question into an availability
question: the hackathon brief mandates an avatar-based product, and a single provider with an
empty balance is a single point of failure eleven days before submission. Anam (`api.anam.ai`)
was tried as a replacement and verified on a live call — [`docs/engineering/anam.md`](../../engineering/anam.md).

The two providers do the same job and share almost nothing structurally. Runway is create → poll
`READY` → `/consume` → a LiveKit room, and its model reaches our tools through an RPC answered by
a hidden participant we join to that room. Anam is one `POST` that returns a JWT, its own WebRTC
signalling, and a model that reaches our tools by calling a URL from Anam's servers.

That last difference is the one with teeth. The gate — the rule that the model may not state a
verdict our process did not give it — was previously enforced by physics: the tools were only
reachable from inside a room nobody else could join. On Anam the same handlers need a public
front door, and Anam signs nothing: its webhook request carries only the arguments the model
extracted, with nothing in it that says whose call it belongs to.

## Decision

Both providers implement the existing `AvatarProvider` and `AvatarRpcHost` ports and are selected
by `AVATAR_PROVIDER`. Everything downstream is shared and unchanged: the credential pool, the
lease, the daily minute budget, the reaper, the waitlist, the brief builder, the tool handlers,
the audit trail and every route.

The client is told exactly one provider fact — `transport` on the grant, `livekit` or `anam` —
because the two speak different wire protocols and there is no honest way to hide that. It picks
a module with it and nothing else; `useAvatarCall`'s interface, the tiers, the copy and the
screens do not change, and neither transport is loaded unless a call uses it.

For Anam the gate becomes a route, `POST /api/v1/avatar/tool/:runwaySessionId/:tool`, authenticated
by a secret minted per call and baked into the persona config's tool headers at session-create
time. A small registry shared by the provider, the gate and the route keeps the original ordering
invariant intact: the provider mints the secret, `open()` attaches the handlers, and a tool call
that arrives before the handlers are attached is refused with a 409 rather than answered.

## Alternatives

- **Client tools relaying to our API.** Identity would come free from the customer's own session
  bearer and no public URL would be needed. Rejected: it moves the gate into the browser, so the
  advice audit trail would record a verdict the client had the opportunity to invent.
- **One pre-created webhook tool per tool name, with the call id as a model-supplied argument.**
  Rejected: identity supplied by the model is identity that can be spoofed across customers.
- **Replacing Runway outright.** Rejected: the adapters already sit behind a port, so keeping both
  costs one `if` in the composition root and buys a second supplier when one runs dry.
- **Renaming `runwaySessionId` throughout.** Deferred: it is now the *call* id under either
  provider, and renaming it touches the lease store, the audit schema, two clients and a public
  route for no behavioural gain.

## Consequences

- `AVATAR_PROVIDER=anam` needs `ANAM_PUBLIC_BASE_URL`, and it cannot be localhost, because the
  webhook is server-to-server. Local development needs a tunnel. Config refuses to start without
  it: tools declared with an unreachable URL would leave the model answering from its own head,
  which is worse than shipping no gate.
- The per-call secret is the whole of the gate's authentication. Anam offers no request signing,
  so the compare is constant-time and the secret is never reused across calls.
- **Anam's transcript carries no tool calls**, where Runway's put them on the assistant turn. The
  reconciler's provider-side evidence is therefore weaker under Anam, and the gate's own ledger
  is the record of what was asked.
- **An abandoned Anam call frees its slot more slowly.** Runway's handle went dark when the room
  died and `sweepDisconnected` released the lease seconds later. Anam reports a closed connection
  to the client only, so teardown falls back to the `/end` beacon and then the reaper at the cap.
  The slot is never leaked, only held longer than it needs to be. *(Amended 2026-09-20: the gate
  now polls `GET /v1/sessions` for our `clientLabel`, so the slot is freed once Anam reports an
  outcome. The beacon and the reaper remain the backstop where nothing is listed.)*
- Uday does not exist on Anam yet: the account's one custom avatar is a woman, the stock faces are
  Western, and the ten stock voices are GB, US and FR. A matching Uday needs a one-shot avatar and
  a cloned voice, the way Runway's was cloned.

## Amendment — 2026-09-20: the interface narrowed, and the liveness made honest

**(1) The narrowed provider interface.** `AvatarProvider` no longer carries Runway's session key
between two of its own calls. `waitUntilReady(cred, id, {timeoutMs}): Promise<{sessionKey}>` and
`consume(id, sessionKey)` have become `awaitIssuable(cred, id, {timeoutMs}): Promise<void>` and
`issueGrant(cred, id)`. Each adapter now keeps whatever it learns while waiting in a private
per-call map — Runway its one-shot `/consume` bearer, Anam its minted JWT — so the step count is
the adapter's business and Anam's `awaitIssuable` is honestly empty rather than a pass-through
that hands back a stored token. Runway deletes its key before spending it, so a retried grant
cannot double-spend the one shot, and `cancel` clears it too. The grant order and the state
machine are unchanged: the gate opens before anything is issued, and `assertConsumable` still
throws from any state but `gated`.

The other half of the same change was deferred here for scope — `@dhan/contracts` was not being
touched — and has since been done. `AvatarSessionOptions.tools` was `RunwayToolDefinition[]`, a
type carrying a dead `type: 'backend_rpc'` field that neither adapter read: `runway/transport.ts`
emitted its own and `toAnamTools` never looked at it. `RunwayToolDefinition` is now
`ToolDefinition`, the `type` field is gone, and the `toRunwayTools` alias for
`toolJsonSchemas()` is deleted (nothing in the workspace called it). `AvatarProvider`,
`toRunwayToolBody` and `toAnamTools` all speak the neutral type, and `type: 'backend_rpc'` is
written in exactly one place, `toRunwayToolBody` in
`apps/api/src/adapters/runway/transport.ts`. The assertion that pinned the discriminator moved
with it rather than being dropped: out of `packages/contracts/src/registry.test.ts`, which no
longer asserts any provider's wire shape, and into
`apps/api/test/avatar/runway-tool-body.test.ts`, which also pins the flatten from JSON Schema to
Runway's parameter list, which nothing covered before.

**(2) The liveness fix, and what it corrects in this ADR.** The last Consequence above said an
abandoned Anam call is freed by the beacon and then the reaper. That was true and it was also
hiding a lie one layer down: `AnamToolGate` returned `connected: true` unconditionally, which did
not merely delay `sweepDisconnected` — it disabled it silently, and the reaper then charged the
full 600 s cap against the daily minute budget for a call that may have lasted thirty seconds.

`RpcHandle.connected: boolean` is replaced by
`AvatarRpcHost.liveness(handle): Promise<'connected' | 'gone' | 'unknown'>`. Runway answers from
the LiveKit connection this process holds, exactly as before. Anam answers from
`GET /v1/sessions`, matching our own `clientLabel` and reading the row's reported outcome — one
briefly cached page read per credential, covering every live call on that key. No row, an
unreadable one, or a transport failure is `unknown`, and `unknown` is never treated as `gone`.
So both failure directions land on the old behaviour — the beacon, then the reaper — rather than
on a torn-down live call. No adapter is now able to claim a liveness it cannot observe.

`DISCONNECT_GRACE_MS` rises from 5 s to 15 s with it. The sweep is no longer a synchronous read
of a held connection: under Anam it is an HTTP round trip plus up to the transport's 4 s cache
staleness, so five seconds could be satisfied by the same cached page read twice.
`test/avatar/room-closed.test.ts` moves with it and now charges 1.8 minutes instead of 1.6 for a
longer simulated call; the behaviour it pins — slot freed after the grace, elapsed minutes
charged rather than the cap — is unchanged.

**What `gone` is allowed to be read off, and why it is narrower than it first was.** The first
cut of this amendment read any non-empty `exitStatus`, or any numeric `sessionLengthMs`, as an
outcome. That guarded one direction and not the other. `exitStatus` and `sessionLengthMs` are
attested only by the API-surface table in `docs/engineering/anam.md` —
`docs/engineering/evidence/anam-call-check.mjs` read only `id` and `clientLabel` — and that
table describes the endpoint as listing PAST sessions, so the shape of a *live* row is
unobserved. If a live row carries `sessionLengthMs` as elapsed-so-far, or
`exitStatus: 'IN_PROGRESS'`, then the first cut sets `gone` on the first poll after the customer
connects and tears the call down about seventeen seconds in, on every Anam call. Refusing to
infer `gone` from absence does not help there: the cost was in the presence direction. And no
test could catch it, because every Anam test stubs `fetch` and therefore asserts against our own
belief about the payload.

So `classify` in `adapters/anam/transport.ts` now acts only on evidence it recognises.
`sessionLengthMs` is not an outcome at all. An `exitStatus` matching a known in-progress
spelling is `live`; one matching a known terminal spelling is `ended`; a spelling in neither set
is `unclear`, which the gate reads as `unknown` and logs at warn with the raw value. A listed
row reporting no outcome at all stays `connected`, as before. The consequence is that if Anam's
real terminal spelling is not in the set, liveness quietly does nothing and we are back to the
beacon and the reaper — the state we were in before this amendment, at no cost to a customer —
and the warn line names the spelling to add. That log line is the cheapest way to confirm the
field on a real call; it should still be confirmed, with the raw row captured, before anyone
relies on the sweep under Anam.

**The liveness read is on its own circuit breaker.** Making it a network call put an internal
housekeeping poll — every two seconds, customer or no customer — behind the same
`CircuitBreaker` that `availability` and `assertUsable` read through
`AnamAvatarProvider.breakerState()`. Anam returning 503 on `GET /v1/sessions` alone would have
tripped it within a handful of sweeps and answered every `POST /api/v1/avatar/session` with
`breaker_open` while token minting was perfectly healthy; and because the list read is not the
unbilled probe, a half-open breaker would then refuse it without running it, so it recorded
neither a failure nor a success and could not close itself. `AnamTransport` now holds a second
breaker, `listBreaker`, that only `listSessions` answers to, and the list read is flagged
`probe` so a half-open list breaker closes itself on the next successful poll. A failing list
endpoint now costs an `unknown` liveness answer and nothing else.

**The sweep does not overlap itself.** `sweepDisconnected` awaits network I/O now, up to the
transport's 8 s call timeout per call, while the interval that drives it fires every 2 s. An
in-flight flag around the interval callback stops three sweeps racing each other onto the same
rate-limited endpoint, and `teardown` deletes the call's `disconnectedSince` entry, which the
sweep itself cannot do for a call that has already left `live`.

**(3) `runwaySessionId` stays.** The deferral recorded in Alternatives above still holds and is
reaffirmed here: the name reaches the lease store, the audit schema, the public route
`/api/v1/avatar/session/:runwaySessionId/*`, the tool-gate route, `AvatarGrant`,
`OperatorAvatarStatus` and the mobile client. Renaming it is a separate change with no
behavioural gain, and nothing in this amendment depends on it.

**(4) `avatarToolCall` stays registered under every provider.** Considered and rejected: gating
the route's registration on `AVATAR_PROVIDER`. The registry is the only way a route exists —
`apps/api/test/contract/no-undeclared-route.test.ts` proves the registered set equals it exactly,
and the OpenAPI document is derived from it — so a config-dependent served surface costs more
than it buys. And it buys almost nothing: an unregistered path answers 404 from the not-found
handler, and the registered one answers 404 through `NullToolWebhook`'s `unknown_call`. The gate
on the active provider already exists at the right seam — the `AvatarToolWebhook` port, chosen
once in the composition root, which is where provider knowledge belongs. The precedent is the
operator group: registered always, 404 without `OPERATOR_KEY`.

## Amendment — 2026-09-22: both at once, as one ordered chain of accounts

The owner's rule for the demo: judges should get Runway's face, because it is the better one, and
Anam only when Runway cannot take the call. Runway allows one live session per account, so the
same rule has to cover capacity as well as credit. `AVATAR_PROVIDER` is therefore no longer a
swap. It is an order: `runway,anam` builds both providers and puts every Runway account ahead of
every Anam one.

- **Accounts are numbered slots.** `RUNWAY_API_KEY_n` / `RUNWAY_CHARACTER_ID_n` and
  `ANAM_API_KEY_n` / `ANAM_AVATAR_ID_n` (+ optional `ANAM_VOICE_ID_n` / `ANAM_LLM_ID_n`, because
  a cloned voice belongs to the account that cloned it). Slot order is try order. The comma lists
  are still read when no slot is numbered, so every deployment pinned to them is unchanged.
- **`AvatarCredential.provider`** says whose key it is, and
  `application/avatar/provider-router.ts` sends each call to that provider's adapter. The session
  service, the lease, the budget, the reaper and the waitlist still see one provider and one RPC
  host. `transport` moved off the provider and onto the grant `issueGrant` returns, because it is
  now a fact about the account that took the call, not about the process.
- **A grant walks the chain inside one request.** The first account that is free (lease), not
  benched and on a live line (breaker) is tried. If it refuses, the attempt is torn down exactly
  as a failed grant always was, and the next account is tried. A refusal that is the account's
  (out of credits, busy with a session we hold no lease for, a rejected key) benches that account
  in `CredentialHealth`: 30 minutes, 1 minute and 10 minutes respectively. A refusal that is the
  provider's (5xx, timeout, breaker open, a gate that would not join) benches nothing. It skips
  that provider's other accounts for the rest of the grant, since they would only prove the same
  thing. Repeated provider failures are the circuit breaker's job across requests.
- **Balances are read, not discovered.** `credits()` joins the port (Runway:
  `GET /v1/organization → creditBalance`, unbilled; Anam: null). `CreditWatch` reads them at
  boot, after every call an account carried, and at most once a minute when the call screen asks
  for availability. Never on the grant itself, because that is the latency the owner cares about
  most. An account under `AVATAR_MIN_CREDITS` (42 by default, two minutes of Runway) is skipped
  before it is asked, and a call's cap is never longer than its account's balance can pay for.
- **A queue that does not move is contention.** `queued: true` is still not a failure on sight,
  because Runway raises it on nearly every first poll. Held for six seconds, it now means the
  account's one slot is taken, and the chain moves on rather than making the customer wait out
  forty-five. READY gets 20 seconds per account, down from 45.

What did not change: the gate opens before any grant, on every provider, in every attempt. The
lifecycle is per attempt, and `assertConsumable` still throws from any state but `gated`.
`test/avatar/failover.test.ts` pins the chain's behaviour and `test/avatar/accounts-config.test.ts`
its spelling in the environment.
