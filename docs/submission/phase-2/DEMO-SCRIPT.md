# The four things that will bite you

## 1. Why these questions and not others

Uday's figures do not come from the model. Every one goes through **`query_spend`** →
`answer()` in `packages/core/src/query.ts`, which is **eight keyword rules, first match
wins**:

```
safe to spend > subscriptions > spending (category word + period) > ULIP/endowment
> cover > debt > why > savings and balances
```

A question that trips none of them returns *"I am not sure what you are asking, and I will
not guess at a number"* — honest, and terrible on video. **Every question in this script was
put to the live API on Rohan and came back `matched: true`, on subject.**

Three traps if you improvise on the day:
- **Words match whole.** "loan" hits, "loan**s**" misses. "SIP" hits, "SIP**s**" misses.
- **Order decides.** Any spending word anywhere makes it a spending question.
  *"savings plan"* is read as the ULIP question, not as savings.
- **"Why" and "explain" always answer with the top finding**, whatever else you asked. For
  Rohan that is the maturing deposit.

### Spare questions, all verified on Rohan

| Ask | He answers |
|---|---|
| *"How much can I spend safely until payday?"* | ₹20,943 — ₹698 a day for 30 days, after ₹51,997 of commitments and ₹10,933 set aside |
| *"How much do I have in savings for an emergency?"* | ₹2,82,448 and ₹2,00,000 in deposits — **₹1,41,663 has not moved in twelve months** |
| *"How much interest do I pay on my debt?"* | ₹39,770 at 9.15%, ₹303 a month — **education loan ends in 5 months, freeing ₹8,200** |
| *"What did I spend on food last month?"* | ₹8,290 across 20 payments in August; mostly Coffee shop ₹3,654 and Swiggy ₹2,773 |
| *"What should I do first, and why?"* | The ₹2,00,000 deposit — *"if you do nothing it auto-renews at the counter rate"* |
| *"Is a Jeevan plan a good way to save?"* | The same refusal, reached by a different product name |

## 2. The Hindi rule

The router is **English regex over Devanagari text.** Tested live:

| Asked in Hindi | Result |
|---|---|
| *"क्या मुझे **ULIP** लेना चाहिए?"* | ✅ **matched** — the Latin token `ULIP` hits the rule |
| *"मेरे सब्सक्रिप्शन पर कितना खर्च होता है?"* | ❌ **missed** — pure Devanagari, falls back |

**The only Hindi question that is safe is the product one — and it is the best one in the
script.** It works by either path: the model calls `check_suitability` with the English
shelf name (the brief hands it the product list in English), so the gate never sees Hindi;
or it routes to `query_spend` and the Latin "ULIP" matches anyway.

**Do not ask beats 10 or 12 in Hindi.** They will fall back, and you will have filmed the
app admitting it does not understand.

## 4. The safety net

If the call drops, or Hindi comes back in English, or he improvises a number — **stop, and
show the fallback ladder instead.** Say it out loud:

> *"The avatar is the face, not the brain. If Uday is busy, the same question, the same
> rules and the same record come back in text — phrased entirely by the engine, with no
> model involved at all."*

Then ask the same ULIP question in **Chat in text** and show the identical refusal. A jury
that watches a system degrade gracefully trusts it more than one that watches a flawless
take.

**The one thing you must not ship:** a take where Uday states a number that is wrong. The
whole claim is *code owns the numbers, the model owns the words.*

# Shooting notes

- **Screen-record a real phone**, or the Expo web target at 390×844 in a thin phone frame
- **Record audio from the tab, not the room mic** — Uday's voice must be clean. Your own
  questions can be slightly roomy; it makes them read as live
- **No music in Part 2.** Music under Part 1 is fine, low, and drops out at 1:57
- **Burn in subtitles for every one of Uday's answers.** Half the jury watches on mute, and
  the refusal is the one thing they must still read
- **Hold every ₹ figure ~2 seconds:** ₹12,411 · ₹27,204 · ₹1,02,00,000 · ₹985
- **One cut per beat.** Thirteen beats, thirteen cuts. Resist a montage
- **3:00 is a hard ceiling.** Over? Cut in this order: Beat 12, then Beat 5, then trim the
  cold open to 6s. **Never cut Beat 11 or Beat 13**

## What to leave out

Real work that does not earn its seconds: challenges, save hacks, the credit view, the
statement enrichment, the AA consent flow, the IDBI sandbox customers. They are slide-10
and slide-13 material. A video that shows everything shows nothing.
